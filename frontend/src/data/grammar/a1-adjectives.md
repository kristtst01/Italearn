# Adjectives, demonstratives & quantities

Adjectives describe things: *piccolo* (small), *stanco* (tired), *italiano*. In Italian they change their ending to match the noun, the way articles do: *un libro piccolo*, *una casa piccola*. This unit covers that agreement, where adjectives go, *questo* and *quello* (this and that), and words for amounts: *molto*, *poco*, *tutto*, *qualche*.

## Agreement

An adjective agrees with its noun in gender (masculine or feminine) and number (singular or plural).

### Adjectives in -o and -e

Most adjectives end in **-o**, and have four forms:

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | *un libro rosso* | *due libri rossi* |
| Feminine | *una casa rossa* | *due case rosse* |

*Rosso* is red.

Adjectives in **-e** have two forms: the same word for masculine and feminine, and **-i** in the plural:

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | *un ragazzo inglese* | *due ragazzi inglesi* |
| Feminine | *una ragazza inglese* | *due ragazze inglesi* |

These work with *essere* too: the adjective agrees with the subject.

> Giulia è stanca. Marco e Paul sono stanchi.
> Giulia is tired. Marco and Paul are tired.

- For a mixed group, use the **masculine** plural: *Marco e Giulia sono stanchi*.
- Spelling works as with nouns: *stanco → stanchi*, *stanca → stanche*, keeping the hard sound. A few in *-co* soften in the masculine plural, like nouns: *simpatico → simpatici* (nice, likeable), but *simpatica → simpatiche*.

Some common adjectives:

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| *grande* | big | *piccolo* | small |
| *nuovo* | new | *vecchio* | old |
| *giovane* | young | *bello* | beautiful, nice |
| *buono* | good | *cattivo* | bad, nasty |
| *facile* | easy | *difficile* | difficult |
| *caro* | expensive, dear | *libero* | free |
| *bianco* | white | *nero* | black |
| *verde* | green | *giallo* | yellow |

### Adjectives that don't change

A few adjectives have only one form. The common ones are the colours **blu** (blue) and **rosa** (pink):

> Una macchina blu. Due macchine blu.
> A blue car. Two blue cars.

## Where adjectives go

Usually the adjective goes **after** the noun:

> Ho una macchina nuova. È un problema difficile.
> I have a new car. It's a difficult problem.

Colours and nationalities go after: *una casa bianca*, *un amico inglese*.

A few short, common adjectives often go **before** the noun: *bello, buono, grande, piccolo, nuovo, vecchio, giovane*. Before the noun they sound more like an opinion; after it, more like a plain description. Both are usually correct:

> Una bella casa. Una casa bella.
> A beautiful house. (both)

With a few adjectives, the position changes the meaning:

| Before | | After | |
|---|---|---|---|
| *un vecchio amico* | an old friend (for years) | *un amico vecchio* | an old friend (old in age) |
| *un povero ragazzo* | a poor boy (you feel sorry for him) | *un ragazzo povero* | a poor boy (without money) |
| *un grande uomo* | a great man | *un uomo grande* | a big man |

For now, put adjectives after the noun, except the ones you've heard before it, like *bella casa*.

### Bello and buono before a noun

Before a noun, **buono** changes like *un*: **buon** before most masculine words, **buono** before s + consonant or z. You know it from *buongiorno*, *buon appetito* and *buon viaggio*:

> Un buon caffè. Una buona idea.
> A good coffee. A good idea.

Before a noun, **bello** changes like the article *il / lo / l'*: *un bel libro*, *un bello zaino*, *un bell'uomo*, *una bella casa*; plural *bei libri*, *begli zaini*, *belle case*. After the noun, or after *essere*, it's regular: *Il libro è bello.*

These shortened forms are worth recognising, but you don't need to produce them yet. If in doubt, put *bello* after the noun.

## Nationalities

Nationalities are adjectives, so they agree: *italiano, italiana, italiani, italiane*; *inglese, inglesi*. Unlike English, they're written with a **small letter**:

> Giulia è italiana. Chloé è francese. Le ragazze sono italiane.
> Giulia is Italian. Chloé is French. The girls are Italian.

The language is the same word, masculine: *l'italiano*, *il francese*. Countries keep their capital letter: *l'Italia*, *la Francia*.

## Questo and quello

**Questo** (this) has the four regular forms, and no article:

> Questo libro. Questa casa. Questi libri. Queste case.
> This book. This house. These books. These houses.

Before a vowel, *questo* and *questa* often shorten: *quest'anno* (this year).

**Quello** (that) changes like the article *il / lo / l'*, just like *bello*:

| | Before | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| Masculine | most words | *quel libro* | *quei libri* |
| Masculine | s + consonant, z | *quello zaino* | *quegli zaini* |
| Masculine | a vowel | *quell'amico* | *quegli amici* |
| Feminine | a consonant | *quella casa* | *quelle case* |
| Feminine | a vowel | *quell'amica* | *quelle amiche* |

On their own, without a noun, both have just the four regular forms: *questo, questa, questi, queste* and *quello, quella, quelli, quelle*.

> Quale borsa vuoi? Quella.
> Which bag do you want? That one.

*Questo* and *quello* never take an article: *questo libro*, never *il questo libro*.

## How much and how many

**Molto** (a lot of, many), **poco** (little, few), **tanto** (so much, so many) and **troppo** (too much, too many) agree like adjectives when they go with a noun: the singular for things you can't count, the plural for things you can.

> Bevo molta acqua. Ho molti amici.
> I drink a lot of water. I have a lot of friends.

> Ho pochi soldi. C'è troppo zucchero.
> I have little money. There's too much sugar.

*Tanti auguri!* (best wishes) is *tanto* in the plural.

With an adjective or a verb, they don't change: *molto bello*, *troppo caro*, *Lavoro molto*. The test is simple: next to a noun they agree; anywhere else they stay *-o*.

> La casa è molto bella. Lavoro troppo.
> The house is very beautiful. I work too much.

**Più** (more) and **meno** (less, fewer) never change: *più tempo* (more time), *meno soldi* (less money), *Lavoro meno* (I work less). With an adjective, *più* makes a comparison: *più grande* (bigger). Comparisons in full come at A2.

### Tutto, ogni and qualche

**Tutto** (all, every, whole) agrees, and takes the article after it:

> Tutto il giorno. Tutta la settimana. Tutti gli studenti. Tutte le sere.
> All day. All week. All the students. Every evening.

**Ogni** (every, each) never changes, and goes with a singular noun: *ogni giorno* (every day). *Ogni giorno* and *tutti i giorni* mean much the same.

**Qualche** (some, a few) never changes either, and also takes a singular noun, though the meaning is plural:

> Ho qualche amico a Roma.
> I have a few friends in Rome.

You know it from *qualche volta* (sometimes).

## Typical mistakes

| ✗ | ✓ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| una macchina rosso | una macchina rossa | The adjective agrees with the noun. |
| due case piccolo | due case piccole | Feminine plural: *-e*. |
| Marco e Giulia sono stanche. | Marco e Giulia sono stanchi. | A mixed group takes the masculine. |
| due macchine blue | due macchine blu | *Blu* doesn't change. |
| Sono Italiano. | Sono italiano. | Nationalities have a small letter. |
| il questo libro | questo libro | No article with *questo* and *quello*. |
| quello libro | quel libro | Before a noun, *quello* changes like *il*. |
| un buono caffè | un buon caffè | Before a noun, *buono* changes like *un*. |
| molto amici | molti amici | Next to a noun, *molto* agrees. |
| qualche amici | qualche amico | *Qualche* takes a singular noun. |
| tutti amici | tutti gli amici | *Tutto* takes the article. |

## Summary

**Agreement.** *-o* adjectives have four forms (*rosso, rossa, rossi, rosse*), *-e* adjectives two (*inglese, inglesi*). A mixed group is masculine. *Blu* and *rosa* never change.

**Position.** Usually after the noun, always for colours and nationalities. Short common ones (*bello, buono, grande, piccolo, nuovo, vecchio*) often go before. *Un vecchio amico* is a friend of many years. Before a noun, *buono* changes like *un* and *bello* like *il*.

**This and that.** *Questo, questa, questi, queste*. *Quello* changes like *il*: *quel, quello, quell', quella, quei, quegli, quelle*. No article with either.

**Amounts.** *Molto, poco, tanto, troppo* agree next to a noun and stay *-o* otherwise. *Tutto* + article; *ogni* and *qualche* + a singular noun.
