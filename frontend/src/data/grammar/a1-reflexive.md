# Reflexive verbs

You've said *Mi chiamo…* since the first chapter. It means "I call myself", and *chiamarsi* is a **reflexive verb**: a verb with a pronoun that points back at the subject. Italian has many of these for everyday routines: getting up, getting dressed, having fun. This unit covers the pronouns, where they go, and the common verbs.

Many of them aren't reflexive in English. *Mi alzo* is just "I get up", and *mi diverto* is "I have fun". Learn them as verbs that happen to come with a pronoun.

## The pronouns

A reflexive verb is an ordinary verb with a pronoun in front that matches the subject. In the dictionary, it ends in **-si**: *chiamarsi*, *alzarsi* (to get up).

| | **alzarsi** (to get up) | |
|---|---|---|
| io | **mi** alzo | I get up |
| tu | **ti** alzi | you get up |
| lui / lei / Lei | **si** alza | he / she gets up, you get up (formal) |
| noi | **ci** alziamo | we get up |
| voi | **vi** alzate | you (plural) get up |
| loro | **si** alzano | they get up |

The verb part is regular: *alzarsi* is an *-are* verb like *parlare*, *mettersi* an *-ere* verb, *vestirsi* an *-ire* verb. Only the pronoun is new, and **si** covers *lui, lei, Lei* and *loro*.

## Common reflexive verbs

| | |
|---|---|
| *chiamarsi* | to be called |
| *alzarsi* | to get up |
| *svegliarsi* | to wake up |
| *lavarsi* | to wash (yourself) |
| *vestirsi* | to get dressed |
| *sentirsi* | to feel |
| *divertirsi* | to have fun, to enjoy yourself |
| *riposarsi* | to rest |
| *fermarsi* | to stop |
| *sposarsi* | to get married |

> Mi sveglio alle sette e mi alzo subito.
> I wake up at seven and get up straight away.

> Come ti senti? Mi sento bene.
> How do you feel? I feel fine.

> Ci divertiamo molto a Roma.
> We're having a lot of fun in Rome.

> La domenica ci svegliamo tardi. I bambini si divertono al parco.
> On Sundays we wake up late. The children have fun in the park.

> Giulia si veste ed esce. Ci fermiamo qui?
> Giulia gets dressed and goes out. Shall we stop here?

*Presto* is early and *tardi* is late: *Mi alzo presto* (I get up early), *Mi sveglio tardi* (I wake up late).

## Where the pronoun goes

The pronoun goes **before** the verb, and *non* goes before the pronoun:

> Non mi alzo presto la domenica.
> I don't get up early on Sundays.

With an **infinitive**, the pronoun is joined to the end of it, and it changes to match the person: *alzarmi*, *alzarti*, *alzarsi*, *alzarci*, *alzarvi*.

With **volere**, **potere** and **dovere**, you can put the pronoun before the modal or join it to the infinitive. Both are correct and equally common:

> Domani mi devo alzare presto. Domani devo alzarmi presto.
> Tomorrow I have to get up early. (both)

> Mi piace svegliarmi tardi.
> I like waking up late.

The pronoun always matches the person: *Mi piace svegliarmi*, *Ti piace svegliarti*. Keeping *-si* (*svegliarsi*) after *mi piace* is a common mistake.

## Reflexive or not

Many reflexive verbs also have an ordinary form without the pronoun. The pronoun is the difference between doing something to yourself and doing it to something else:

> Lavo la macchina. Mi lavo.
> I'm washing the car. I'm washing (myself).

> Chiamo Marco. Mi chiamo Marco.
> I'm calling Marco. My name is Marco.

For your own body, Italian uses the reflexive with the article, not *my*:

> Mi lavo le mani.
> I wash my hands.

### Each other

In the plural, **ci**, **vi** and **si** can also mean "each other":

> Marco e Giulia si conoscono. Ci vediamo domani!
> Marco and Giulia know each other. See you tomorrow!

*Ci vediamo* (literally "we'll see each other") is the everyday way to say "see you".

## Typical mistakes

The common one is **leaving out the pronoun**: *Alzo alle sette* instead of *Mi alzo alle sette*, especially with verbs that aren't reflexive in English, like *alzarsi* and *divertirsi*. When a verb's dictionary form ends in *-si*, the pronoun always comes with it.

The other two are about where it goes. *Non* comes before the pronoun (*Non mi alzo*, not *Mi non alzo*), and a pronoun joined to an infinitive matches the person: *Mi piace svegliarmi*, not *svegliarsi*.

## Summary

**Forms.** *Mi, ti, si, ci, vi, si* + the ordinary verb: *mi alzo, ti alzi, si alza, ci alziamo, vi alzate, si alzano*. In the dictionary, the verb ends in *-si*.

**Position.** Before the verb, with *non* before the pronoun. Joined to an infinitive and matching the person (*alzarmi*). With modals, either place: *mi devo alzare* or *devo alzarmi*.

**Meaning.** Often not reflexive in English (*mi diverto*, I have fun). Without the pronoun, the verb acts on something else (*lavo la macchina*). Your body: *mi lavo le mani*. In the plural, also "each other": *ci vediamo*.
