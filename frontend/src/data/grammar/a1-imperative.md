# The imperative

The imperative is for telling someone to do something: *Guarda!* (Look!), *Aspettate!* (Wait!), *Non parlare!* (Don't talk!). At A1 you need it for *tu* and *voi*, the people you'd give instructions to in everyday life. For *Lei*, the polite form, you'll learn a handful of set phrases you've partly met already, like *scusi*.

## Tu and voi

| | **guardare** | **prendere** | **sentire** | **finire** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tu | **guarda** | **prendi** | **senti** | **finisci** |
| voi | **guardate** | **prendete** | **sentite** | **finite** |

*Finire* is to finish.

- The **voi** form is the same as the present: *guardate, prendete, sentite*.
- The **tu** form is the same as the present for *-ere* and *-ire* verbs: *prendi, senti, finisci*.
- The one change: for **-are** verbs, the *tu* form ends in **-a**, not *-i*: *guarda*, *parla*, *aspetta*, *ascolta*.

> Guarda! Aspetta un momento. Ascoltate, per favore.
> Look! Wait a moment. Listen, please.

> Prendi un caffè? Prendi il treno delle otto.
> Are you having a coffee? Take the eight o'clock train.

The second pair shows why the *-ere* forms can look like questions: *prendi* is both "you take" and "take!". Your voice, and the situation, tell them apart.

**For "let's"**, use the *noi* form of the present: *Andiamo!* (Let's go!), *Mangiamo!* (Let's eat!).

## Irregular forms

A few common verbs have a short *tu* form. Each has a longer form too, the same as the present, and both are correct:

| | Short | Long | |
|---|---|---|---|
| *andare* | **va'** | *vai* | go |
| *fare* | **fa'** | *fai* | do, make |
| *dare* | **da'** | *dai* | give |
| *stare* | **sta'** | *stai* | stay, be |
| *dire* | **di'** | | say, tell |

The apostrophe marks the missing letter, so *va'* is written with one, never *va*. *Dire* has only the short form, *di'*.

> Va' a casa! Sta' attento!
> Go home! Be careful!

*Essere* and *avere* have their own forms: **sii** and **siate** (be), **abbi** and **abbiate** (have). The common one is *sii*: *Sii gentile!* (Be kind!). *Venire* and *uscire* are regular here: *vieni, venite*; *esci, uscite*.

## Don't: negative commands

For **voi**, just put *non* in front: *Non parlate!* (Don't talk!).

For **tu**, use *non* + the **infinitive**:

> Non parlare! Non aspettare! Non andare via!
> Don't talk! Don't wait! Don't go away!

*Via* after a verb of movement means "away": *andare via* (to go away).

This is the one part of the imperative that doesn't come from the present, and a common mistake: *Non parla!* is "he isn't talking", not "don't talk".

## With pronouns

With a reflexive verb, the pronoun is **joined to the end** of a command:

> Alzati! Svegliati! Divertiti!
> Get up! Wake up! Have fun!

> Alzatevi! Sbrigatevi!
> Get up! Hurry up! (to several people)

*Sbrigarsi* is to hurry up.

With the short forms, the first letter of the pronoun doubles: *di'* + *mi* = **dimmi** (tell me), *fa'* + *mi* = **fammi** (let me, make me). *Dimmi!* is very common, as "Tell me" or "Go on".

In a negative, the pronoun can go before or after: *Non ti preoccupare* and *Non preoccuparti* both mean "Don't worry". *Preoccuparsi* is to worry.

## Formal set phrases

For *Lei*, the imperative uses different forms, which come later. At A1, learn these as fixed phrases:

| | |
|---|---|
| *Scusi!* | Excuse me! |
| *Senta!* | Excuse me! (to get attention; literally "listen") |
| *Mi dica.* | Tell me, how can I help? |
| *Prego, si accomodi.* | Please, take a seat. |
| *Prenda pure.* | Go ahead, take it. |
| *Venga!* | Come! |

> Scusi, senta: dov'è la stazione?
> Excuse me: where's the station?

*Mi dica* is what you'll hear in a shop or at a counter, the way an English speaker might say "What can I get you?"

## Is it rude?

Among friends and family, a plain command is normal, and *per favore* makes it friendly: *Passa il sale, per favore* (Pass the salt, please). *Passare* is to pass.

When you're asking for something, a question in the present is often softer, the way English uses "Can you…?":

> Mi passi il sale? Mi dai una mano?
> Can you pass me the salt? Can you give me a hand?

With strangers, staff and anyone you call *Lei*, ask rather than tell: *Vorrei un caffè*, or *Scusi, mi dà il conto?* (Excuse me, can I have the bill?). *Dà* is the *Lei* form of *dare*, the same as *lui dà*.

You'll also hear the imperative of *dare* everywhere as a word of its own: **Dai!** means "Come on!", to encourage someone or to say "you're kidding".

## Typical mistakes

| ✗ | ✓ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Parli! (to a friend) | Parla! | *-are* verbs: *tu* ends in *-a*. |
| Non parla! (to a friend) | Non parlare! | Negative *tu*: *non* + infinitive. |
| Non vai via! (to a friend) | Non andare via! | Negative *tu*: *non* + infinitive. |
| Va a casa! | Va' a casa! / Vai a casa! | The short form has an apostrophe. |
| Sei gentile! | Sii gentile! | "Be" is *sii*. |
| Di mi! | Dimmi! | Joined, with a double *m*. |

## Summary

| | *-are* | *-ere* | *-ire* | Don't |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tu | *guarda* | *prendi* | *senti*, *finisci* | *non guardare* |
| voi | *guardate* | *prendete* | *sentite*, *finite* | *non guardate* |

Short forms: *va', fa', da', sta', di'*; *sii, abbi*. Pronouns join the end: *alzati, dimmi*. "Let's": *andiamo*. For *Lei*: *scusi, senta, mi dica*, or ask with a question.
