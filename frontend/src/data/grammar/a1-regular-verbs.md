# The present tense: regular verbs

So far you've used two verbs, *essere* and *avere*. This unit gives you all the others that follow the regular patterns: how to say *I speak*, *you read*, *we sleep*. Once you know the three patterns, you can use hundreds of verbs.

The next unit covers the common verbs that don't follow the patterns (*fare*, *andare*, *volere* and others).

## The three verb groups

The dictionary form of a verb, the **infinitive**, ends in **-are**, **-ere** or **-ire**. That ending tells you which pattern the verb follows:

| Group | Infinitive | Meaning | Stem |
|---|---|---|---|
| -are | **parlare** | to speak | parl- |
| -ere | **prendere** | to take | prend- |
| -ire | **dormire** | to sleep | dorm- |

To make the present tense, take off the *-are*, *-ere* or *-ire* to get the **stem**, then add the ending for the person. As with *essere* and *avere*, the ending already says who is doing it, so the pronoun is usually left out: *Parlo italiano* (I speak Italian).

## -are verbs

The biggest group by far.

| | parlare | |
|---|---|---|
| io | **parlo** | I speak |
| tu | **parli** | you speak |
| lui / lei / Lei | **parla** | he / she speaks, you (formal) speak |
| noi | **parliamo** | we speak |
| voi | **parlate** | you (plural) speak |
| loro | **parlano** | they speak |

Common -are verbs:

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **abitare** | to live (somewhere) | **lavorare** | to work |
| **studiare** | to study | **mangiare** | to eat |
| **ascoltare** | to listen to | **aspettare** | to wait for |
| **arrivare** | to arrive | **comprare** | to buy |
| **guardare** | to watch, look at | **cercare** | to look for |
| **pagare** | to pay | **chiamare** | to call |

> Abito a Roma e lavoro in centro.
> I live in Rome and I work in the centre.

Two spelling details:

- **-care and -gare** add an **h** before *i*, to keep the hard sound: *cercare → cerchi, cerchiamo*; *pagare → paghi, paghiamo*.
- **-iare** verbs don't double the *i*: *studiare → studi* (not *studii*), *mangiare → mangi, mangiamo*.

**Stress.** In the *loro* form the stress stays on the stem, not on the ending: ***par**lano*, ***la**vorano*. The same goes for the other groups: ***pren**dono*, ***dor**mono*.

## -ere verbs

| | prendere | |
|---|---|---|
| io | **prendo** | I take |
| tu | **prendi** | you take |
| lui / lei / Lei | **prende** | he / she takes, you (formal) take |
| noi | **prendiamo** | we take |
| voi | **prendete** | you (plural) take |
| loro | **prendono** | they take |

Common -ere verbs:

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **leggere** | to read | **scrivere** | to write |
| **vivere** | to live | **vedere** | to see |
| **chiudere** | to close | **rispondere** | to answer |
| **mettere** | to put | **credere** | to believe |

> Prendo un caffè e leggo.
> I'm having a coffee and reading.

**Sound, not spelling.** In verbs like *leggere*, the *g* is hard before *o* and soft before *i* and *e*: *leggo* (hard), *leggi*, *legge* (soft). The spelling stays the same; only the sound changes.

*Prendere* also means *to have* for food and drink: *Prendo un caffè* (I'll have a coffee).

## -ire verbs

| | dormire | |
|---|---|---|
| io | **dormo** | I sleep |
| tu | **dormi** | you sleep |
| lui / lei / Lei | **dorme** | he / she sleeps, you (formal) sleep |
| noi | **dormiamo** | we sleep |
| voi | **dormite** | you (plural) sleep |
| loro | **dormono** | they sleep |

Common -ire verbs like this: **partire** (to leave), **aprire** (to open), **sentire** (to hear, to feel), **offrire** (to offer).

Notice how close *-ere* and *-ire* are: only the *voi* form differs (*prendete*, *dormite*).

## -isc- verbs

Many -ire verbs add **-isc-** in four of the six forms:

| | finire | |
|---|---|---|
| io | **finisco** | I finish |
| tu | **finisci** | you finish |
| lui / lei / Lei | **finisce** | he / she finishes, you (formal) finish |
| noi | **finiamo** | we finish |
| voi | **finite** | you (plural) finish |
| loro | **finiscono** | they finish |

- The **-isc-** goes in the *io*, *tu*, *lui / lei* and *loro* forms; *noi* and *voi* are regular.
- You've already met one: *Non **capisco*** (I don't understand), from **capire**.
- Others: **preferire** (to prefer), **pulire** (to clean), **spedire** (to send).
- The infinitive doesn't show whether a verb takes *-isc-*, so learn it with the verb: *capire (capisco)*.

**Sound.** *Sc* is hard before *o* (*capi**sco***: "sk") and soft before *i* and *e* (*capi**sci***, *capi**sce***: "sh").

## Using the present

The Italian present covers several English forms:

> Leggo un libro.
> I read a book. / I'm reading a book.

> Domani parto.
> I'm leaving tomorrow.

- **I do / I'm doing:** one form for both. Don't add *essere*: *Studio italiano*, never *Sono studio*.
- **The near future:** with a time word, the present says what's going to happen: *Domani lavoro* (I'm working tomorrow).
- **Questions and negatives** work as with *essere* and *avere*: *Parli inglese? Non parlo tedesco.*

## Typical mistakes

| ✗ | ✓ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Io parla italiano. | Io parlo italiano. | The *io* form ends in *-o*. |
| Loro parlono. | Loro parlano. | -are verbs: *-ano*. |
| Loro prendano. | Loro prendono. | -ere and -ire verbs: *-ono*. |
| Voi dormete. | Voi dormite. | -ire verbs: *-ite*. |
| Non capo. | Non capisco. | *Capire* takes *-isc-*. |
| Tu cerci. | Tu cerchi. | -care verbs add *h* before *i*. |
| Tu studii. | Tu studi. | -iare verbs don't double the *i*. |
| Sono studio italiano. | Studio italiano. | The present needs no *essere*. |

## Summary

| | -are | -ere | -ire | -isc- |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| io | -o | -o | -o | -isco |
| tu | -i | -i | -i | -isci |
| lui / lei / Lei | -a | -e | -e | -isce |
| noi | -iamo | -iamo | -iamo | -iamo |
| voi | -ate | -ete | -ite | -ite |
| loro | -ano | -ono | -ono | -iscono |

- **Stem + ending:** *parl-o*, *prend-i*, *dorm-e*. The pronoun is usually dropped.
- **Spelling:** *-care / -gare* add *h* before *i* (*cerchi*); *-iare* keeps one *i* (*studi*).
- **-isc-** in *io*, *tu*, *lui / lei* and *loro*: *capisco, capisci, capisce, capiscono*.
- **One present for English's two:** *leggo* = I read / I'm reading. With a time word, it's also the near future.
