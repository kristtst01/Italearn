# Questions, negation & linking

You've been asking questions since the first chapter: *Come stai?*, *Di dove sei?*, *Quanti anni hai?* This unit puts the question words together, explains the order words go in, and covers the negatives: *non*, and words like *mai* (never) and *niente* (nothing) that work together with it. It ends with how often you do things, and the small words that join sentences (*e*, *ma*, *perché*, *quando*).

## Yes-or-no questions

As you saw with essere, a yes-or-no question has the same words as a statement. Your voice rises at the end, and in writing you add a question mark:

> Marco abita a Roma. Marco abita a Roma?
> Marco lives in Rome. Does Marco live in Rome?

There's no *do* or *does*, and nothing changes place. The words can still move around for emphasis: *Abita a Roma, Marco?* is the same question, with Marco said almost as an afterthought.

## Question words

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **che cosa**, **cosa**, **che** | what | *Che cosa fai stasera?* |
| **chi** | who | *Chi è?* |
| **come** | how, what … like | *Come stai?* *Com'è Roma?* |
| **dove** | where | *Dove abiti?* |
| **quando** | when | *Quando parti?* |
| **perché** | why | *Perché studi italiano?* |
| **quale**, **quali** | which, what | *Quale libro? Qual è il problema?* |
| **quanto**, **quanta**, **quanti**, **quante** | how much, how many | *Quanto costa? Quanti anni hai?* |

*Stasera* is this evening, tonight.

**What: che cosa, cosa or che.** All three are correct. *Che cosa* is the neutral choice. *Cosa* is very common in speech, especially in the north, and *che* on its own is common in the centre and south: *Cosa fai? Che fai?*

**Which: quale or che.** Before a noun, *che* is the everyday word and *quale* is a little more formal: *Che libro leggi?* and *Quale libro leggi?* both mean "Which book are you reading?" *Quale* has a plural, *quali*. Before *è*, it shortens to **qual è**, written without an apostrophe.

**How much, how many: quanto** agrees with the noun after it, like an adjective: *quanto tempo*, *quanta acqua*, *quanti anni*, *quante sorelle*. On its own, *quanto* means "how much": *Quanto costa?*

**Perché** means both "why" and "because", so the answer can start with the same word as the question:

> Perché studi italiano? Perché lavoro a Roma.
> Why are you studying Italian? Because I work in Rome.

**Dove**, **come** and **cosa** often join with *è*: *dov'è*, *com'è*, *cos'è*.

> Cos'è? Com'è Firenze?
> What is it? What's Florence like?

### Word order with a question word

The question word comes first. When the subject is a noun or a name, it usually goes at the end:

> Dove abita Marco? Quando arriva il treno?
> Where does Marco live? When does the train arrive?

You'll also hear the subject at the start, as a topic: *Marco, dove abita?* Both are correct.

### Prepositions come first

In English, a preposition can sit at the end of a question: "Who are you going out *with*?" In Italian it goes at the front, before the question word:

> Con chi esci stasera?
> Who are you going out with tonight?

> Di dove sei? Da dove vieni?
> Where are you from? Where do you come from?

> Per chi è il caffè? A chi scrivi?
> Who's the coffee for? Who are you writing to?

**Di chi** asks whose something is, from *di* for owners:

> Di chi è il telefono? È di Giulia.
> Whose phone is it? It's Giulia's.

## Negatives

You know the main rule: **non** goes straight before the verb.

> Non lavoro oggi.
> I'm not working today.

There's no *don't*, *doesn't* or *isn't*: *non* does all of that. A short answer is often *no* followed by the full negative: *Lavori oggi? No, non lavoro.*

### Never, nothing, nobody

Words like *mai* (never) and *niente* (nothing) are negatives too. In English you'd use only one negative in a sentence ("I never drink coffee"). In Italian, when the negative word comes **after the verb**, *non* stays before it, so the sentence has two:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **non … mai** | never | *Non bevo mai caffè.* |
| **non … niente**, **non … nulla** | nothing, not anything | *Non faccio niente.* |
| **non … nessuno** | nobody, not anybody | *Non c'è nessuno.* |
| **non … più** | not any more, no longer | *Non studio più.* |
| **non … neanche** | not even, not … either | *Non ho neanche un euro.* |

*Niente* and *nulla* mean the same; *niente* is more common in speech.

When the negative word comes **before the verb**, at the start of the sentence, *non* is left out:

> Nessuno parla inglese qui.
> Nobody speaks English here.

> Niente è facile.
> Nothing is easy.

**Nessuno** before a noun works like *un*: *nessun problema* (no problem), *nessuna domanda* (no questions). It's always singular.

**Neanche** on its own means "neither": *Neanche io* (me neither) is the negative partner of *anche io* (me too).

In questions, *nessuno* can mean "anybody". You'll hear it at a door or on the phone:

> C'è nessuno?
> Is anybody there?

In casual speech some people drop the *non* in a few phrases, like *fa niente* (never mind). In standard Italian, and in anything you write, keep it: *Non fa niente.*

## How often

| | |
|---|---|
| **sempre** | always |
| **spesso** | often |
| **di solito** | usually |
| **qualche volta**, **a volte** | sometimes |
| **mai** | never (*non … mai*) |

*Sempre*, *spesso* and *mai* usually go straight after the verb. *Di solito* and *qualche volta* usually go at the start:

> Mangio sempre a casa. Vado spesso al cinema.
> I always eat at home. I often go to the cinema.

> Di solito prendo il treno. Qualche volta vado in macchina.
> I usually take the train. Sometimes I go by car.

## Joining sentences

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **e** | and | *Studio e lavoro.* |
| **o** | or | *Un caffè o un tè?* |
| **ma**, **però** | but | *È piccolo, ma è bello.* |
| **perché** | because | *Studio perché lavoro in Italia.* |
| **quando** | when | *Quando sono stanco, dormo.* |
| **se** | if | *Se hai tempo, andiamo al cinema.* |
| **anche** | also, too, even | *Anche Marco viene.* |

*Bello* is beautiful, nice.

- **E** often becomes **ed** before a word starting with *e*, so two vowels don't run together: *ed è*. Both are correct; *ed* is common in writing.
- **Ma** and **però** both mean "but". *Ma* is the everyday one; *però* is a little stronger, like "though" or "however", and can also go at the end: *È piccolo. È bello, però.*
- **Anche** goes before the word it's about: *Anche Marco viene* (Marco's coming too), *Marco viene anche domani* (Marco's coming tomorrow too). That's why it's *anche io*, never *io anche*.

> Se hai fame, c'è un panino in cucina.
> If you're hungry, there's a sandwich in the kitchen.

## Typical mistakes

| ✗ | ✓ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Bevo mai caffè. | Non bevo mai caffè. | After the verb, *mai* needs *non* too. |
| Faccio niente. | Non faccio niente. | After the verb, *niente* needs *non* too. |
| Non nessuno parla inglese. | Nessuno parla inglese. | Before the verb, no *non*. |
| Chi esci con? | Con chi esci? | The preposition goes first. |
| Quanto sorelle hai? | Quante sorelle hai? | *Quanto* agrees with the noun. |
| Qual'è il problema? | Qual è il problema? | *Qual è* has no apostrophe. |
| Perchè studi italiano? | Perché studi italiano? | *Perché* has an acute accent. |
| Io anche. | Anche io. | *Anche* goes before the word it's about. |

## Summary

**Questions.** Same words as a statement, with a rising voice. With a question word, it goes first and a noun subject usually goes last: *Dove abita Marco?* A preposition goes before the question word: *Con chi? Di dove? Di chi?* *Quanto* agrees (*quanti anni, quante sorelle*); *qual è* has no apostrophe; *perché* is why and because.

**Negatives.** *Non* before the verb. With *mai, niente, nulla, nessuno, più, neanche* after the verb, keep *non* as well: *Non bevo mai caffè.* At the start of the sentence, drop it: *Nessuno parla inglese.*

**How often.** *Sempre, spesso, mai* after the verb; *di solito, qualche volta* at the start.

**Joining.** *E* (*ed*), *o*, *ma* / *però*, *perché*, *quando*, *se*, and *anche* before the word it's about.
