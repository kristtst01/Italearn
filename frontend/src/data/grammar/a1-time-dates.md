# Time & dates

This unit covers the calendar and the clock: first, second and third; the days of the week, the months and the seasons; dates; and telling the time. It uses the numbers to 100 and the joined prepositions you already know: *alle tre* is just *a* + *le*.

## First, second, third

Ordinal numbers say the order of things. The first ten have their own forms:

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | *primo* | 6th | *sesto* |
| 2nd | *secondo* | 7th | *settimo* |
| 3rd | *terzo* | 8th | *ottavo* |
| 4th | *quarto* | 9th | *nono* |
| 5th | *quinto* | 10th | *decimo* |

From 11th on, drop the last vowel of the number and add **-esimo**: *undici → undicesimo*, *venti → ventesimo*, *cento → centesimo*.

Ordinals are adjectives, so they agree, and they usually go before the noun:

> La prima volta. Il secondo giorno. Le prime settimane.
> The first time. The second day. The first weeks.

*Volta* is time, as in "one time, two times": *la prima volta*, *qualche volta*.

## Days of the week

| | |
|---|---|
| *lunedì* | Monday |
| *martedì* | Tuesday |
| *mercoledì* | Wednesday |
| *giovedì* | Thursday |
| *venerdì* | Friday |
| *sabato* | Saturday |
| *domenica* | Sunday |

- Days are written with a **small letter**, unlike in English.
- They're masculine, except **la domenica**. The ones ending in *-ì* don't change in the plural: *i lunedì*.
- The week starts on Monday.

For "on Monday", Italian uses no preposition. With no article, it means one particular day; with the article, it means every week:

> Lunedì vado a Roma. Il lunedì lavoro a casa.
> On Monday I'm going to Rome. On Mondays I work from home.

> Che giorno è oggi? Oggi è giovedì.
> What day is it today? Today is Thursday.

## Months and seasons

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| *gennaio* | January | *luglio* | July |
| *febbraio* | February | *agosto* | August |
| *marzo* | March | *settembre* | September |
| *aprile* | April | *ottobre* | October |
| *maggio* | May | *novembre* | November |
| *giugno* | June | *dicembre* | December |

Months take a small letter too. "In May" is **a maggio** or **in maggio**; both are correct. Before a month starting with *a*, **a** usually becomes **ad**, so the two vowels don't run together: *ad aprile*, *ad agosto*.

> Ad agosto vado al mare.
> In August I'm going to the seaside.

The seasons take an article: **la primavera** (spring), **l'estate** (summer, feminine), **l'autunno** (autumn), **l'inverno** (winter). "In summer" is **in estate** or **d'estate**; the same works for *inverno*: *in inverno*, *d'inverno*. For the others, use *in*: *in primavera*, *in autunno*.

## Dates

A date is **il** + a number + the month. The number is the ordinary one, except for the first of the month, which uses **primo**:

> Il primo maggio. Il due maggio. Il ventuno giugno.
> The first of May. The second of May. The twenty-first of June.

Before a number that starts with a vowel, *il* becomes **l'**: *l'otto marzo*, *l'undici settembre*.

> Oggi è il tre maggio. Il compleanno di Giulia è il dodici marzo.
> Today is the third of May. Giulia's birthday is the twelfth of March.

*Compleanno* is birthday.

To ask the date: *Che giorno è oggi?* asks the day of the week or the date. *Quanti ne abbiamo oggi?* asks only the date. Answer with the date: *Oggi è il tre.*

Written in numbers, the day comes first: *3/5* is the third of May, not the fifth of March.

## Telling the time

To ask the time, both **Che ora è?** and **Che ore sono?** are correct and common.

The answer uses *sono le* + the hour. *Sono le tre* is literally "they are the three", with *ore* (hours) understood: the hours are plural and feminine, so the verb is *sono* and the article is *le*. One o'clock is a single hour, so it's **è l'una**, literally "it is the one":

> Sono le tre. Sono le dieci. È l'una.
> It's three o'clock. It's ten o'clock. It's one o'clock.

Midday and midnight have no article: **è mezzogiorno**, **è mezzanotte**.

Minutes go after the hour with **e** (and) or **meno** (minus):

| | Literally | In English |
|---|---|---|
| *Sono le tre e dieci.* | the three and ten | It's ten past three. |
| *Sono le tre e un quarto.* | the three and a quarter | It's a quarter past three. |
| *Sono le tre e mezza.* | the three and a half | It's half past three. |
| *Sono le quattro meno un quarto.* | the four minus a quarter | It's a quarter to four. |
| *Sono le quattro meno cinque.* | the four minus five | It's five to four. |

*E mezza* and *e mezzo* are both correct; *e mezza* is more common. *Un quarto* is a quarter.

**Timetables** use the 24-hour clock, read out as numbers: *Il treno parte alle diciotto e trenta* (The train leaves at 18:30). In conversation, people usually say *alle sei e mezza*.

### At what time

"At" is **a** + the article, so the joined forms come back: **alle** for most hours, **all'una**, and **a mezzogiorno**, **a mezzanotte** with no article.

> A che ora parti? Alle otto.
> What time are you leaving? At eight.

> Pranzo all'una. Ceno alle otto e mezza.
> I have lunch at one. I have dinner at half past eight.

From and to use **dalle** and **alle**:

> L'ufficio è aperto dalle nove alle cinque.
> The office is open from nine to five.

*Pranzare* is to have lunch, *cenare* to have dinner, and *aperto* means open. All the verbs are regular.

**Roughly and exactly.** *Verso le otto* is "around eight", and *alle otto in punto* is "at eight sharp". *Fra* (in) works with time too, as you saw with prepositions: *fra dieci minuti* (in ten minutes).

### Morning, afternoon, evening

To make clear which part of the day you mean, add **di mattina**, **del pomeriggio** (in the afternoon), **di sera** or **di notte** (at night):

> Alle tre del pomeriggio. Alle dieci di sera.
> At three in the afternoon. At ten in the evening.

For habits, use the article on its own, like with the days: *la mattina* (in the mornings), *il pomeriggio*, *la sera*.

> La mattina bevo un caffè. La sera guardo la tv.
> In the morning I have a coffee. In the evening I watch TV.

## Typical mistakes

| ✗ | ✓ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Lunedì, 3 Maggio | lunedì 3 maggio | Days and months have a small letter. |
| l'uno maggio | il primo maggio | The first of the month uses *primo*. |
| È le tre. | Sono le tre. | The hours are plural. |
| Sono le una. | È l'una. | One o'clock is singular. |
| alle mezzogiorno | a mezzogiorno | Midday has no article. |

## Summary

| | |
|---|---|
| What time is it? | *Che ora è? / Che ore sono?* |
| It's three. It's one. It's midday. | *Sono le tre. È l'una. È mezzogiorno.* |
| 3:15, 3:30, 3:45 | *le tre e un quarto, le tre e mezza, le quattro meno un quarto* |
| At three. At one. At midday. | *alle tre, all'una, a mezzogiorno* |
| From nine to five | *dalle nove alle cinque* |
| On Monday / on Mondays | *lunedì / il lunedì* |
| In May, in summer | *a maggio, in estate* |
| The 1st, the 2nd of May | *il primo maggio, il due maggio* |

Days and months take a small letter; *primo, secondo, terzo* agree like adjectives, and from 11th on they end in *-esimo*.
