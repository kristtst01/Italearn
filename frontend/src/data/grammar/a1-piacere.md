# Piacere & indirect pronouns

*Piacere* is how Italian says "to like", but it's built the other way round from English. Italian doesn't say "I like coffee"; it says *Mi piace il caffè*, literally "coffee is pleasing to me". So the thing you like decides the verb, and the person who likes it is a pronoun in front.

## How piacere works

Compare the two languages:

| English | Italian | Literally |
|---|---|---|
| I like coffee. | *Mi piace il caffè.* | To me is pleasing the coffee. |
| I like books. | *Mi piacciono i libri.* | To me are pleasing the books. |

- The thing you like is the **subject**, so the verb agrees with it: **piace** for one thing, **piacciono** for more than one.
- The person who likes it is an **indirect pronoun** before the verb: *mi* (to me), *ti* (to you), and so on.
- The thing usually takes the article, because it's meant in general: *il caffè*, *i libri*, *la musica*.

> Mi piace la musica. Mi piacciono i film italiani.
> I like music. I like Italian films.

Almost everything you'll say uses just these two forms, *piace* and *piacciono*. You'll also hear *Mi piaci* (I like you), where "you" is the subject.

## Who likes it: the pronouns

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **mi** | to me | *Mi piace il tè.* I like tea. |
| **ti** | to you | *Ti piace il tè?* Do you like tea? |
| **gli** | to him | *Gli piace il tè.* He likes tea. |
| **le** | to her | *Le piace il tè.* She likes tea. |
| **Le** | to you (*Lei*) | *Le piace il tè?* Do you like tea? |
| **ci** | to us | *Ci piace il tè.* We like tea. |
| **vi** | to you (*voi*) | *Vi piace il tè?* Do you (plural) like tea? |
| **gli** | to them | *Gli piace il tè.* They like tea. |

- **Gli** means both "to him" and "to them". In formal writing you'll also see *loro* after the verb for "to them" (*piace loro*), but in speech *gli* is normal.
- **Le** is "to her", and also "to you" for *Lei*, just as *Lei* is the same word as *lei*.
- In casual speech, many people say *gli* for "to her" too. In writing, use *le*.

These pronouns go before the verb. To make it negative, *non* goes before the pronoun:

> Non mi piace il caffè. Non gli piacciono i gatti.
> I don't like coffee. He doesn't like cats.

### A Marco piace

With a name or a noun, use **a**: *a Marco*, *a Giulia*, *ai bambini*. The *a* takes the place of the pronoun:

> A Marco piace il calcio. A Giulia piacciono i gatti.
> Marco likes football. Giulia likes cats.

There's also a stronger form, **a me**, **a te**, **a lui**, **a lei**, **a noi**, **a voi**, **a loro**, for contrast or emphasis:

> A me piace il tè, a lui piace il caffè.
> I like tea, he likes coffee.

That's why agreeing is **anche a me** (me too), not *anche io*, and **neanche a me** (me neither):

> Mi piace Roma. Anche a me!
> I like Rome. Me too!

Use one or the other, *mi* or *a me*, not both. *A me mi piace* is common in casual speech, but avoid it in writing.

## Piacere with a verb

*Piacere* + an infinitive says what you like doing. The verb is always **piace**, even with two activities:

> Mi piace leggere. Mi piace cucinare e ballare.
> I like reading. I like cooking and dancing.

> Ti piace viaggiare?
> Do you like travelling?

## Liking people

With people, be careful. *Mi piace Marco* usually means you find Marco attractive, and *Mi piaci* (I like you) is what you'd say on a date. To say you like a friend or a colleague, Italian uses *simpatico*:

> Marco mi è simpatico. Giulia mi sta molto simpatica.
> I like Marco. I really like Giulia.

*Mi sta simpatico* is the more casual of the two. *Simpatico* agrees with the person, like any adjective.

## Preferire

**Preferire** (to prefer) is an ordinary *-isc-* verb, so the person is the subject, as in English:

> Preferisco il tè. Preferisci leggere o guardare la tv?
> I prefer tea. Do you prefer reading or watching TV?

*Preferisco*, *preferisci*, *preferisce*, *preferiamo*, *preferite*, *preferiscono*. It goes well with *piacere*:

> Mi piace il caffè, ma preferisco il tè.
> I like coffee, but I prefer tea.

## Typical mistakes

| ✗ | ✓ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Io piace il caffè. | Mi piace il caffè. | The person is *mi*, not *io*. |
| Mi piace i libri. | Mi piacciono i libri. | Plural thing: *piacciono*. |
| Marco piace il calcio. | A Marco piace il calcio. | With a name, *a*. |
| Mi piacciono leggere e cucinare. | Mi piace leggere e cucinare. | With verbs, always *piace*. |
| Mi piace Roma. Anche io! | Mi piace Roma. Anche a me! | Agreeing: *anche a me*. |
| A me mi piace il tè. | Mi piace il tè. / A me piace il tè. | One or the other, not both. |
