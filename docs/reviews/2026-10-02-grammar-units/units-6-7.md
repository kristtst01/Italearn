# Review: Regular verbs (unit 6) and Irregular verbs & modals (unit 7)

Checked every Italian sentence, rule and exercise in both readings and practice files. Conjugation tables, -isc- verb list (capire, finire, preferire, pulire, spedire), -care/-gare spelling, and the hard/soft sound notes (leggo/leggi, conosco/conosci, capisco/capisci) are all correct.

## a1-regular-verbs

- **Location:** "-are verbs", the **Stress** paragraph (line 61)
  - **Severity:** error. **Confidence:** high.
  - **Problem:** `***la**vorano*` bolds the wrong syllable. *Lavorano* is stressed la-VÒ-ra-no, on the same syllable as *lavòro*. The rule as worded ("the stress stays on the stem") is too vague to be useful: *lavor-* is all stem, so it doesn't tell the learner *which* stem syllable. The real rule is that the loro form takes its stress from the io form, and that's exactly what English speakers get wrong (they say la-vo-RA-no, a-bi-TA-no).
  - **Fix:** `**Stress.** The *loro* form is stressed on the same syllable as the *io* form, never on the ending: ***par**lo → **par**lano*, *la**vo**ro → la**vo**rano*, ***a**bito → **a**bitano*. The same goes for the other groups: ***pren**dono*, ***dor**mono*.`
  - **Source:** Accademia della Crusca, "Vademecum sull'accento" (https://accademiadellacrusca.it/it/consulenza/vademecum-sullaccento-quando-indicarlo-e-dove-pronunciarlo/34), example *io àbrogo … loro àbrogano*.

- **Location:** "-are verbs", spelling details, the -iare bullet (and the Summary and Typical mistakes rows "-iare keeps one i" / "Tu studii")
  - **Severity:** should fix. **Confidence:** high.
  - **Problem:** The rule is stated as an absolute, but it only holds when the *i* is unstressed in the io form (*stùdio → studi*). When that *i* is stressed (*scìo, invìo*), the tu form keeps both: *sciare → scii*, *inviare → invii*. *Sciare* is everyday vocabulary that learners will meet soon.
  - **Fix:** `- **-iare** verbs usually don't double the *i*: *studiare → studi* (not *studii*), *mangiare → mangi, mangiamo*. (A few verbs where the *i* is stressed, like *sciare*, keep both: *tu scii*.)` In the Summary, change to "*-iare* usually keeps one *i*".
  - **Source:** standard conjugation of *inviare/sciare* (tu *invii*, *scii*). Treccani's grammar states the same rule for verbs in -iare with a stressed *i*. I confirmed this through search results, not a direct Treccani page fetch.

- **Location:** "Using the present", bullet "I do / I'm doing: one form for both"; Summary "One present for English's two"
  - **Severity:** should fix. **Confidence:** high.
  - **Problem:** This leaves out *stare* + gerund (*sto studiando*, "I'm studying right now"). It's very common in speech, and learners will hear it constantly. As written, the rule implies Italian has no progressive. Note that exercise p-30 uses *Sono studiando* as a distractor, so learners will meet this construction anyway.
  - **Fix:** add after the bullet: `Italian also has a form for "right at this moment" (*Sto studiando*, from *stare*), which comes in a later unit. The simple present is always correct, and it's what Italians use most.`
  - **Source:** Treccani, "strutture perifrastiche" (https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/strutture-perifrastiche_(Enciclopedia-dell'Italiano)/).

- **Location:** a1-regular-verbs-m-07 (`Io ___ un caffè.`, offrire)
  - **Severity:** minor. **Confidence:** medium.
  - **Problem:** *Io offro un caffè* is correct but unnatural without an indirect object. An Italian would say *Offro io* ("it's on me") or *Ti offro un caffè*.
  - **Fix:** `Oggi ___ io il caffè.` (answer *offro*), or `Il caffè lo ___ io.` if the clitic is acceptable. If neither works, use a different -ire verb.

- **Location:** a1-regular-verbs-p-35 accepted answers
  - **Severity:** minor. **Confidence:** high.
  - **Problem:** *noi abitiamo a Roma* is accepted but *noi viviamo a Roma* isn't.
  - **Fix:** add `"noi viviamo a Roma"`. Do the same for m-16 (`"noi abitiamo a Milano da un anno"`, `"noi viviamo a Milano da un anno"`), and for p-37 add `"Io lavoro a Milano."`.

- **Location:** "How long: the present with da", the per sentence
  - **Severity:** minor. **Confidence:** medium.
  - **Problem:** *Studio a Roma per un mese* is fine as a plan ("I'm studying in Rome for a month"). The explanation "the whole length of a period" is accurate, but a learner may then use *per* + present for a finished period. Italian uses a past tense for that, which comes later. This is a small clarity point, not an error.
  - **Fix (optional):** add `(a planned or total period)` after "a one-month stay".

- **Location:** a1-regular-verbs-p-40, distractor `Sono a Roma tre mesi.`
  - **Severity:** minor. **Confidence:** medium.
  - **Problem:** The distractor is wrong as written, which is fine. But it sits one word away from the correct *Sono a Roma da tre mesi*, and the learner is never told that *essere* + *da* also works. No change is needed. Optionally, swap it for `Abito a Roma tre mesi.` so it doesn't suggest that *sono a Roma* is the problem.

**Overall (regular verbs):** The unit is solid. Every conjugation, accent and spelling is correct, the -isc- list is accurate, the hard/soft *g* and *sc* notes are right, and the *da* + present section is accurate and well judged. There is one real error: the stress example *lavorano*, and the stress rule should be rephrased so it is actually usable. Two rules need a short qualifier to be "simple, but true": -iare spelling (*scii*) and the missing *stare* + gerund. The exercises are clean and unambiguous. The distractors in p-17, p-30, p-31, p-39, p-40 and m-15 are wrong in the intended reading. Only small accepted-answer gaps remain.

## a1-irregular-verbs

- **Location:** "Per + infinitive" (the sentence "Italian can't leave out per…"), the Typical mistakes row `Vado a Roma vedere Marco`, and exercises **a1-irregular-verbs-p-33**, **p-35**, **m-15**
  - **Severity:** error (for the exercises), should fix (for the reading). **Confidence:** high.
  - **Problem:** After motion verbs (*andare, venire, uscire*), Italian expresses purpose with **a** + infinitive just as often as *per*, and usually more naturally: *Vado a casa a dormire*, *Vado a Roma a trovare Marco*. The reading itself teaches *Vado a mangiare* in the andare section. Yet:
    - p-33 (`Vado a Roma ___ vedere Marco.`) accepts only *per*, though *a* also fits.
    - p-35 and m-15 accept only *Vado a casa per dormire*. A native speaker would most likely say *Vado a casa a dormire*, and that answer would be marked wrong.
  - **Fix:**
    - In the reading, add: `After *andare* and *venire*, you can also use **a**: *Vado a casa a dormire*, *Vado a Roma a trovare Marco*. *Per* works after any verb.`
    - p-33: make `correct_answer` `["per", "a"]`, or use a non-motion verb, e.g. `Studio italiano ___ lavorare a Roma.`
    - p-35: add `"Vado a casa a dormire."`.
    - m-15: add `"vado a casa a dormire"`.
    - Better still, build the per exercises around non-motion verbs (*studiare, lavorare, comprare*), where *per* really is the only option.
  - **Source:** Treccani, "verbi di movimento" (https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/verbi-di-movimento_(Enciclopedia-dell'Italiano)/): "Unito con un complemento di fine (*a* o *per* + infinito), *andare* ha anche il significato di «spostarsi con un fine determinato»". Also Treccani, "finali, frasi": implicit purpose clauses take "*per* e *a*".

- **Location:** "Venire and uscire", the **Venire da** paragraph
  - **Severity:** should fix. **Confidence:** high.
  - **Problem:** "*Sono di Londra* and *Vengo da Londra* mean the same" is fine for cities, but it misleads in two ways:
    1. *Essere di* + place only works with cities and towns. With a country you say *Sono inglese* or *Vengo dall'Inghilterra*, never *Sono di Inghilterra*. A learner will generalise from London to England.
    2. *Vengo da Londra* can also mean "I'm coming (travelling) from London" right now.
  - **Fix:** `**Venire da** is the other way to say where you're from: *Sono di Londra* and *Vengo da Londra* both mean "I'm from London" (with *venire* it can also mean you're arriving from there). *Essere* takes **di**, *venire* takes **da**. Both work with cities; for countries, use the nationality for now: *Sono inglese*.`

- **Location:** a1-irregular-verbs-p-34 (`Studio italiano per lavorare in Italia`), and p-31 (`vorrei parlare con Giulia`)
  - **Severity:** should fix. **Confidence:** high.
  - **Problem:** Both are type_answer exercises that require the learner to *produce* prepositions the course hasn't taught yet: *in* (+ country) and *con*. The brief says exercises shouldn't depend on them.
  - **Fix:**
    - p-34: change the prompt to `Say 'I'm studying Italian to work in Rome.'` and the answers to `["studio italiano per lavorare a Roma", "studio l'italiano per lavorare a Roma"]`. Update m-14 to match if you want consistency (there *in* is only in context, so that one is acceptable).
    - p-31: change to `Say 'I'd like to eat an ice cream.'` → `["vorrei mangiare un gelato"]`, or keep it and add `"vorrei parlare a Giulia"` as also correct. Because *con* is untaught, the prompt is better replaced.

- **Location:** a1-irregular-verbs-p-38 (`Say 'They're going out tonight.'`)
  - **Severity:** minor. **Confidence:** medium.
  - **Problem:** This requires producing *stasera*, a time expression from a later unit. It appears in the reading, so it may be acceptable. Flagged in case the time-expression rule is strict.
  - **Fix (if needed):** `Say 'They're going out.'` → `["escono", "loro escono"]`, or keep *stasera* given in the prompt as a hint.

- **Location:** "Fare, andare and stare", example `Che lavoro fai? Faccio la spesa. Fa freddo.`
  - **Severity:** minor. **Confidence:** high.
  - **Problem:** Laid out on one line with one translation line, it reads as a question and its answer ("What do you do? I do the shopping."), which is a non-sequitur. Also, *Che lavoro fai?* is literally "What job do you do?"
  - **Fix:** split it into separate examples, or translate it as `What's your job? / I do the shopping. / It's cold.` with a separator, e.g. `> Che lavoro fai? · Faccio la spesa. · Fa freddo.`

- **Location:** "Sapere, conoscere and potere", example `Oggi non posso nuotare: ho freddo`
  - **Severity:** minor. **Confidence:** medium.
  - **Problem:** It's grammatical, but "I can't swim today because I'm cold" is an odd reason. The point is "not able / not allowed right now", which a clearer reason shows better.
  - **Fix:** `*Oggi non posso nuotare: devo lavorare* (I can't swim today: I have to work).` This uses only material from this unit.

- **Location:** "Volere, potere and dovere", the Volere + noun bullet
  - **Severity:** minor. **Confidence:** medium.
  - **Problem:** This is accurate. But a learner may over-apply it and avoid *Vuoi un caffè?* when offering something, which is perfectly natural (p-22 uses it). Exercise p-26 also presents *Vorresti un gelato?* as *the* answer for offering to a friend, while *Vuoi un gelato?* is at least as common. There is no exercise bug, because *Vuoi* isn't a distractor.
  - **Fix (optional):** add `Offering something is different: *Vuoi un caffè?* is friendly and normal.` Also, *Prendo un caffè* from the previous unit is a natural way to order, and could be mentioned next to *vorrei*.

- **Location:** Summary, "The noi and voi forms are often regular"
  - **Severity:** minor. **Confidence:** medium.
  - **Problem:** The voi forms here are all regular, but many noi forms in this unit aren't (*facciamo, diciamo, sappiamo, dobbiamo, vogliamo, possiamo, beviamo*). "Often" is true, but readers may take it as reliable.
  - **Fix:** `**The voi form is almost always regular** (*andate, uscite, venite, potete*), and so are many noi forms (*andiamo, usciamo, veniamo*).`

**Overall (irregular verbs):** All conjugation tables are correct, including the accents (*dà*, *può*), and *do* is rightly unaccented. The sapere/conoscere/potere section is accurate and well hedged, and the vorrei, vorresti and vorrebbe usage is right. The main problem is *per* + infinitive. The reading and three exercises (p-33, p-35, m-15) treat *per* as mandatory after *andare*, where *a* + infinitive is equally correct and often more natural. As a result, native-like answers (*Vado a casa a dormire*) get marked wrong. This is the most important fix across both units. The *venire da* / *essere di* equivalence needs a "cities only" qualifier. Two type_answer exercises require prepositions the course hasn't taught yet (*in*, *con*). The other points are small naturalness tweaks. The distractors are wrong in the intended sense in every MC exercise, and the conjugation fill-ins are unambiguous.
